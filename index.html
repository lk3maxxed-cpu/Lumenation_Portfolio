Exhibition 01 / Sculpture & moving image

TAME JOY

Thundershark GalleryWashington, DCOctober 2–31, 2026

Fragmented memory.
Haunted nostalgia.

TAME JOY is an exhibition of fragmented memory and haunted nostalgia. It follows a pathway carved by impulse, imagination, and play as these things turn into value, commodity, and a fetishization of the past.

At its core, the show is a reanimation of something that was once living. A kind of necromancy brought on by the age of gacha games, collectible obsession, and the AI slopification of nostalgic images. Characters, memories, and familiar forms return as things to collect, possess, and encounter again.

Read the full exhibition text

The series exists as an archive and a container for my own memories, social memetic phenomena, and previous shows and works I have performed over the years. It includes returning characters from World_Type and The Ballad of Bunko, brought together here in new physical and digital forms.

The sculptures, or “trophies” as I call them, are synthesized using Blender and Meshy, printed in PLA, and painted by hand. My work is intended to evoke awe through scale and emotion through the intimacy of hand-painted digital forms.

Each character is also represented digitally through a “pseudo-sloptimized” animation. These animations involve roughly ten different modes of process, moving between physical space and digital capture.

TAME JOY brings these processes and histories together through the pleasure of making, the obsession of collecting, and the strange feeling of bringing something back.

Welcome to TAME JOY. I hope you enjoy the show!

Artist Statement

Lumenation Industry is a transcendental media archive and creative studio operating at the intersection of folk art, mass media mythology, and emergent production technology. Under the working identity Lumen the Sim, I develop an ongoing body of work that synthesises a lifetime of media consumption — animation, gaming, devotional imagery, internet culture, and popular iconography — into an original system of visual world-building with its own lore, characters, and cosmology.

My practice draws equally from art historical tradition and the vernacular forms of contemporary life: the social media reel, the fan page, the gif, the meme. I treat these not as low forms but as the folk media of the present moment — the altars, illuminated manuscripts, and morality plays of now. The work moves between handmade fabrication, digital animation, sculptural installation, and moving image, refusing the boundary between the artisanal and the algorithmic.

The figures and worlds I build — among them the pilgrimage of The Ballad of Bunko and the animated vaudeville universe of The Slanted Grove — are conceived as the beginnings of a mythology capable of growing to the scale of the properties that shaped me: Disney, Pokemon, the great popular imaginaries of the twentieth century. I build this alone, using 3D printing, AI-assisted design, and application automation not as novelties but as the home production methods of a new kind of studio — one person competing on imagination rather than budget.

This work is new and growing. It is meant to disseminate globally, to be felt before it is understood, and to prove that the distance between a single maker and an enduring world is shorter than the culture industry would have you believe.
